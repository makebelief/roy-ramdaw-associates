# What should employees and employers know about retrenchment in South Africa?

Retrenchment is a dismissal linked to an employer's operational requirements. It can arise when a business changes how it works or says it can no longer sustain particular roles. The process involves more than announcing a decision: South Africa's Labour Relations Act requires consultation before a contemplated dismissal for operational reasons.

## Consultation starts before a final decision

Section 189 of the Labour Relations Act identifies who must be consulted, depending on collective agreements, workplace forums, trade unions and the employees affected. It requires a meaningful joint consensus-seeking process. The parties should discuss ways to avoid dismissals, reduce their number, change their timing and mitigate their impact. Selection methods and severance pay are also matters for consultation.

The employer must issue a written notice inviting consultation and disclose relevant information. Employees and representatives should keep the notice, proposals, meeting records, written questions and responses. A proposed restructure and the legal adequacy of its process depend on the particular workplace and evidence.

## Questions worth asking

- What operational reasons are given, and what information supports them?
- Which employees or representatives must be consulted?
- Have alternatives to dismissal been considered seriously?
- How will roles and employees be selected, and are the criteria fair and objective?
- What notice, severance and other payments might apply under the law, contract or collective agreement?

Different rules can apply to larger retrenchments under section 189A. A later dispute may concern substantive fairness, procedural fairness, payments or more than one issue. The proper forum and time limit depend on the claim. Obtain case-specific advice promptly if a deadline may be running.
