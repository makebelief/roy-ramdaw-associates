# What constitutes unfair dismissal in South Africa?

An employee who has lost a job may ask whether the dismissal was fair. The answer depends on the reason for ending employment, the process followed and the evidence. South Africa's Labour Relations Act (LRA) recognises the right not to be unfairly dismissed. It also defines “dismissal” more broadly than a simple letter ending a permanent contract.

## Start with what happened

Under section 186 of the LRA, dismissal can include termination by an employer, certain failures to renew a fixed-term contract, refusal to allow an employee to resume work after protected leave, and resignation where the employer made continued employment intolerable. That last category is often called constructive dismissal. Each category has specific legal requirements; a difficult workplace alone does not automatically prove constructive dismissal.

Sections 188 and 192 address fairness and the burden of proof. In broad terms, the employer must be able to show a fair reason and fair procedure once dismissal is established. Reasons may concern conduct, capacity or operational requirements. A dispute can be about the reason, the process, or both. Whether the dismissal is *automatically unfair* is a separate legal question under section 187.

## Check the referral date promptly

Section 191 generally requires a dispute about the fairness of a dismissal to be referred within 30 days of dismissal or the employer's later final decision to uphold it. The correct recipient may be a bargaining council or the CCMA. A late referral may need condonation; it is unsafe to assume an extension will be granted. Do not wait for a website article to decide a deadline.

Keep the contract, payslips, dismissal letter, hearing notices, minutes, relevant messages and a dated chronology. Those records help an attorney assess the reason, procedure, forum and possible remedy. Conciliation usually comes before arbitration or Labour Court proceedings, but the route depends on the claim.
