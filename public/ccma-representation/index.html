LABOUR & EMPLOYMENT LAW

CCMA Advice and Representation

The Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) deals with many workplace disputes, but jurisdiction, time limits and representation rules vary. Roy Ramdaw & Associates Inc. assists with CCMA and bargaining council processes and can explain how those rules apply to a proposed referral or existing matter.

← All practice areas

Before and during conciliation

An initial review should identify the dispute, the correct forum and the relevant deadline. The CCMA’s published rules explain how to serve and file a referral and what documents may be required. Conciliation seeks to resolve the dispute. Preparation can include a clear chronology, key records and a realistic view of possible settlement, even where an attorney may not appear at the hearing.

  • Check referral forms and proof of service
  • Organise contracts, payslips, notices and correspondence
  • Prepare the main facts and desired resolution

If the dispute remains unresolved

Some disputes proceed to arbitration after a certificate of non-resolution or under a combined conciliation-arbitration process. Others are for the Labour Court. The firm can help assess the route, prepare evidence and submissions, and advise on the steps following an award or ruling. Statutory and rule-based time limits should be checked for the specific matter.

  • Identify the appropriate next forum
  • Preserve documents and witness details
  • Review the applicable deadlines and hearing notice

A note on attorney appearances

Legal representation is not automatic at every CCMA stage. The CCMA rules place particular limits on attorney representation in some dismissal arbitrations; an agreement or commissioner’s permission may be required. Ask the firm to check the current rule and case category before assuming an attorney can appear.
