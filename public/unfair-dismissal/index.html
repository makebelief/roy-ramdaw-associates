LABOUR & EMPLOYMENT LAW

Unfair Dismissal Advice and Representation

A dismissal dispute calls for a prompt review of what happened, why employment ended and which process applies. Roy Ramdaw & Associates Inc. advises on unfair dismissal matters as part of its established employment practice. The firm can assess the documents and explain possible referral, conciliation, arbitration or court steps for the particular case.

← All practice areas

What needs to be assessed?

The Labour Relations Act protects employees from unfair dismissal. A dispute may concern the reason for dismissal, the procedure followed, or both. The legal definition of dismissal also covers some less obvious situations, including certain fixed-term contract disputes and constructive dismissal. A legal assessment depends on the actual employment relationship and evidence.

  • Dismissal letter and stated reasons
  • Contract, policies, hearing notices and minutes
  • Messages, witness details and a dated sequence of events

Time limits and the right forum

Under section 191 of the Labour Relations Act, a dispute about the fairness of a dismissal is generally referred within 30 days of dismissal or a later final decision to uphold it. Depending on sector and jurisdiction, referral may be to a bargaining council or the CCMA. Late referrals may need a condonation application; do not assume extra time will be granted.

  • Confirm the date from which time runs
  • Check whether a bargaining council has jurisdiction
  • Seek advice promptly if the deadline may have passed

How the firm can assist

The firm can review the merits, prepare for conciliation, assess whether arbitration or Labour Court proceedings are appropriate and advise on possible remedies. Representation at a CCMA hearing is governed by the CCMA rules; an attorney’s ability to appear depends on the type of dispute and the commissioner’s ruling where required. No outcome can be promised.
